# Image-Gen Prompts

Concept image only; not fabrication authority. Do not include cut-ready dimensions, hole locations, or toolpaths.

## Hero Prompt

A single finished "duntong," an original cylindrical wooden tongue drum standing upright like a short barrel: a tall round drum body built from many narrow vertical Padauk staves glued into a seamless cylinder, the wood a warm reddish-orange with subtle vertical grain lines marking the stave seams, satin oil finish catching the light. Around the middle of the cylinder, encircling it like a belt, runs a single ring of eight slender wooden tongues cut directly into the curved shell wall — each tongue a long narrow rectangular cutout, fixed at one end and free at the tip, the tongues stepping shorter in sequence around the circumference to suggest a tuned melodic scale, thin dark slit gaps outlining each tongue against the body. The top end shows a recessed solid wooden end cap; the form reads as a hand-drum shell that has been turned smooth and true on a lathe. Realistic table-height scale, roughly a foot in diameter. Clean neutral studio background, soft directional studio light raking across the curved staves to show the cylinder's roundness and the relief of the cut tongues, 3/4 product-photography angle, ~50mm lens, high detail, photorealistic, no text labels.

## In-Context Prompt

Two duntong drums staged together on a warm wooden workshop bench near a window — the larger medium floor-size cylinder standing on the bench and the smaller handheld version resting beside it, both turned from reddish-orange Padauk staves with the signature ring of eight cut tongues around each shell equator. A pair of soft felt-tipped mallets and a few wood shavings rest nearby, hinting at a recently completed build. Natural side light from the window, shallow depth of field with the smaller drum softly out of focus, environmental storytelling of a maker's shop, concept visualization only, photorealistic, no text labels.

## Detail Prompt

Macro close-up of the signature tongue ring on the curved Padauk shell: three or four adjacent cantilever tongues cut into the rounded wall, each a long slender rectangle anchored at its base and free at the tip, separated from the body by a clean narrow dark slit that wraps slightly with the cylinder's curvature. Show the fine vertical grain of the reddish-orange wood, the faint glue seams between staves crossing the tongues, the crisp routed edges of the slits, and the satin sheen of an oil finish. Raking low-angle light emphasizes the slight relief of each tongue and the depth of the cut gaps. High detail, photorealistic, no measurements, no callouts.

## Negative / Avoid

No text/logos/watermarks/fake maker marks, no dimension callouts or measurement lines, no impossible mechanisms, no flat rectangular box tongue-drum lid (tongues must wrap a round shell), no metal hardware on the tongues; anatomically plausible hands if a player is shown.
